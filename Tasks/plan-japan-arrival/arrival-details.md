## 1. Can you enter Japan? (Visa + Visit Japan Web)

Think of this in two layers: (A) legal permission (visa rules) and (B) speed pass (Visit Japan Web).

### A. Visa rules for you (U.S. passport, short stay)

For U.S. citizens visiting Japan as tourists:

- Japan allows **visa‑free** entry for short stays (up to 90 days) if you are just visiting (no paid work).[6]
- You need:
  - A valid U.S. passport that covers your full stay in Japan.[7][6]
  - A return or onward ticket (your flight home or to another country).[6][7]
  - The address of where you’re staying (your hostels/hotel and sister’s address if needed).[8][7]

You **do not** need to apply for a separate tourist visa in advance if you’re just visiting and have no serious criminal or immigration history.[6]

### B. Visit Japan Web: optional “fast lane”

Japan uses an online system called **Visit Japan Web** that lets you pre‑fill immigration and customs forms and get QR codes you show on arrival.[9][10][11]

Idea: Instead of filling paper forms on the plane while tired, you do it once online, get QR codes, and then breeze through the lines.

High‑level steps (do this when you have Wi‑Fi, takes ~15–20 minutes):

1. **Create an account**  
   - Go to the Visit Japan Web site, enter email + password, confirm email.[10][11][9]

2. **Add your personal details**  
   - In “Your details,” enter: name as on passport, date of birth, passport number, nationality.[11][9][10]

3. **Register your trip (Planned Entry/Return)**  
   - Click something like “Registering planned entry/return” → “New registration.”[10][11]
   - Enter:
     - Arrival date in Japan (April 3, local time).  
     - Airline: Etihad Airways.  
     - Flight number: **800** (they only want the number).[10]
     - First address in Japan: K’s House Tokyo Oasis, 14‑10 Asakusa 2‑chome, Taito‑ku, Tokyo.[2][3][8]

4. **Immigration section**  
   - Answer questions about purpose of visit (tourism), length of stay, where you’re staying.[9][10]

5. **Customs declaration**  
   - Answer questions about how much cash you carry, items you’re bringing, etc.[9][10]

6. **Get QR codes**  
   - When you’re done, the system gives you three QR codes: one for immigration, one for customs, one for duty‑free.[9][10]
   - Screenshot them to your phone.  

If you’re tired or overwhelmed: you can **skip Visit Japan Web** and just fill paper forms on the plane; it just may be a bit slower on the ground.[9]

Let’s break time travel down.

  - **Arrival NRT**: around 11:40–12:35 **next day** local Japan time.[1]

- Add flight duration (≈9.5 hours) + the 5‑hour time difference → arrival about **14.5 hours** after local departure time, on the **calendar next day**.[12][1]

## 3. From Narita (NRT) to your first hostel (K’s House, Asakusa)

You land midday at Narita. You want to end up at **Quality Hostel K’s House Tokyo Oasis** in Asakusa.[3][2]

Think of it as: Airport → **train or bus to Tokyo** → short subway or walk → hostel.

### A. Know the exact hostel location

- K’s House Tokyo Oasis address: **14‑10 Asakusa 2‑chome, Taito‑ku, Tokyo**.[2][3]
- It’s about a **3‑minute walk from Asakusa Station** and 6 minutes from Senso‑ji Temple.[2]
- Area: **Asakusa**, a classic old‑Tokyo neighborhood (temple, old streets, lots of food).[2]

### B. Easiest options from Narita to Asakusa (for you, jet‑lagged with luggage)

1. **Train combo: Narita → Ueno → Asakusa**  
   Best balance of cost + simplicity.

   - Step 1: From Narita, follow signs to JR trains.  
   - Step 2: Take either:
     - **Keisei Skyliner** to Ueno (fast but a bit more expensive), or  
     - Regular **Keisei Limited Express** to Asakusa‑subway‑side (cheaper, slower).  
   - Step 3: From Ueno, hop on Tokyo Metro Ginza Line to **Asakusa Station** (G line, end of the line).  
   - Step 4: Exit the station and walk ~3 minutes to the hostel using the address above; they have an access map explaining routes from Asakusa station.[3]

2. **Limousine bus + subway**  
   This is more “no thinking” but involves a subway at the end.

   - Step 1: At Narita, buy a ticket for **Airport Limousine Bus** to “Tokyo Station / Nihonbashi / TCAT” area (any central‑Tokyo bus that gets you into the city).[13][14]
   - Step 2: From the bus drop‑off, navigate (Google Maps) to a subway station that connects to **Asakusa** (e.g., Toei Asakusa Line or Ginza Line).  
   - Step 3: Ride to Asakusa Station and walk to the hostel.[3][2]

3. **Train directly on Toei Asakusa Line**  
   Some Keisei trains run through directly to Asakusa (Toei Asakusa Line). If you see a through‑train to **Asakusa** on the Toei Asakusa Line from Narita, that’s the simplest: sit down, wait until “Asakusa,” get off, walk 3 minutes.[15][16][3]

If you want minimal decisions when tired:  
- Buy **Suica/PASMO** or use a contactless card (if supported) so you can just tap in/out on trains (Tokyo transit cards). Many guides recommend this for tourists.[17]

## 4. From your hostel to Nest Hotel (next day)

You’re going from Asakusa → **Nest Hotel Tokyo Hanzomon**.

### A. Where is Nest Hotel?

- Name: **Nest Hotel Tokyo Hanzomon** (there’s also a Nest Hotel Tokyo Yaesu; yours sounds like Hanzomon).[18][19]
- Address: **2‑18 Hayabusacho, Chiyoda‑ku, Tokyo 102‑0092**.[4][5][18]
- Neighborhood: **Hanzomon / Chiyoda** – central Tokyo, very close to the Imperial Palace and government offices, quiet at night but easy to reach other areas.[5][4]

### B. Getting there from Asakusa (simple subway route)

Think: Asakusa (northeast, old town) → central Tokyo (Hanzomon).

One straightforward subway pattern:

1. From Asakusa Station (Tokyo Metro Ginza Line), ride toward **Shibuya** direction.  
2. Transfer to the **Hanzomon Line** at a hub station (like Mitsukoshimae or Otemachi).  
3. Take the Hanzomon Line to **Hanzomon Station**.  
4. Nest Hotel Tokyo Hanzomon is about a **1‑minute walk** from Hanzomon Station exits 5 or 6.[18]

Exact transfer point can be chosen by Google Maps on the day; just plug “K’s House Tokyo Oasis” → “Nest Hotel Tokyo Hanzomon.”

## 5. Neighborhoods, other hotels, and “robot/capsule” options

### A. Where you’re staying

- **K’s House Tokyo Oasis**: Asakusa, Taito‑ku. Classic tourist area, temples, street food, lots of hostels, fairly budget friendly.[3][2]
- **Nest Hotel Tokyo Hanzomon**: Hanzomon, Chiyoda‑ku. More business/government, near Imperial Palace, quiet, central.[4][5]

They’re not “walking distance”; think **30–40 minutes by subway** depending on route, not side‑by‑side neighborhoods.

### B. Cheaper / similar options nearby

In **Asakusa**, around K’s House, you’ll find:

- Many budget hostels and guesthouses around Senso‑ji, often similarly priced or a bit cheaper, but K’s House has good reviews as a clean, reliable backpacker brand.[20][2]

In **central Tokyo** near Hanzomon:

- Nest Hotel Hanzomon is already a budget/mid‑range business hotel in central Tokyo. Reviews note clean rooms but compact space, typical for Tokyo.[5][4]
- Around Hanzomon and nearby Kudanshita/Jimbocho you can find other business hotels; prices vary with date, but Nest tends to be competitive.[4][5]

For capsule hotel “experience,” common clusters are:

- **Shinjuku**: several capsule hotels like Shinjuku Kuyakusho‑mae Capsule Hotel, Booth Netcafe & Capsule, etc., near nightlife.[21][22]
- **Akihabara, Nippori, Kinshicho** and others also have capsules.[22][23][21]

These are not right next to Hanzomon, but <30 minutes by train. To actually book or check **tonight’s availability**, you’d open Booking.com/Expedia/etc., pick “capsule hotel” and your exact date; availability changes hour by hour, so you need to check live yourself.[24][21][22]

### C. “Robot hotel” (Henn na Hotel)

Tokyo has **Henn na Hotel** branches (the robot‑staffed “strange hotel”) like **Henn na Hotel Tokyo Hamamatsucho** in central Tokyo near Hamamatsucho Station and Tokyo Tower.[25][26]

- Great if you want the “robot check‑in” experience.  
- Not in Asakusa or Hanzomon; more near Tokyo Tower / Hamamatsucho. Expect ~30 minutes by train from your areas.  

Again, availability must be checked in real time on hotel booking sites; I can’t see live inventory.

## 6. Red‑light district (Kabukicho) and how far it is

Tokyo’s main red‑light/very intense nightlife area:

- Name: **Kabukicho**, in **Shinjuku**.[27][28][29]
- Location: just north of Shinjuku Station East Exit; cross Yasukuni‑dori and you’re there.[27]

From your places:

- From **Hanzomon** to Shinjuku: Hanzomon Line + JR transfer, around 20–25 minutes.  
- From **Asakusa** to Shinjuku: Ginza Line/Toei Asakusa Line + JR, roughly 30–40 minutes.  

Safety / best practices for Kabukicho:

- It’s visually amazing (lights, bars, people watching) but has more scams and aggressive touts than most of Tokyo.[28][27]
- Simple rules:
  - Ignore street touts and “free drinks” offers; go only to places you picked yourself.  
  - Watch your tab; avoid bars with no clear menu/prices.  
  - Stay aware if you’re drinking; stick to main streets, not sketchy alleys.

## 7. What to do on arrival so everything goes smoothly

Think of arrival as a checklist you can literally run through:

### A. On the plane before landing

- Fill in paper immigration/customs forms **or** have your Visit Japan Web QR codes ready (screenshots).[11][10][9]
- Keep your **passport**, boarding pass, and accommodation addresses accessible (not buried in your bag).  

### B. At Narita Airport

1. **Immigration**  
   - Follow “Immigration / Passport Control” signs.  
   - Show your passport and any forms/QR codes.  
   - They’ll ask: purpose of visit (tourism), length of stay, first address. Answer simply.  

2. **Baggage claim**  
   - Follow your flight’s carousel sign, grab your luggage.  

3. **Customs**  
   - Choose green channel if you have nothing to declare; show customs QR or paper form.  

4. **Money + SIM / eSIM**  
   - If you don’t already have  consider eSIM or airport Wi‑Fi/E‑SIM counters.[17]
   - ATMs (7‑Eleven, Japan Post) can usually take international cards. Get some yen cash for small shops and trains.  

5. **Transport into Tokyo**  
   - Decide: **train** vs **airport limo bus** (see Section 3).  
   - Use Google Maps with live directions from “Narita Airport Terminal 1” to “K’s House Tokyo Oasis.”  

### C. Reaching K’s House and settling in

- At **Asakusa Station**, follow maps to “K’s House Tokyo Oasis” (3‑minute walk).[2][3]
- Check in, drop bags, shower, hydrate.  
- Walk to **Senso‑ji Temple** (6 minutes) for an easy, low‑stress first evening: food streets, photos, atmosphere.[2]

### D. Next day: getting to Nest Hotel and meeting your sister

- Take subway from Asakusa → Hanzomon (see Section 4).  
- Walk 1–3 minutes from Hanzomon Station Exit 5/6 to Nest Hotel Tokyo Hanzomon.[5][18][4]
- Check in, then you both can reach other parts of Tokyo easily (Shibuya, Shinjuku, etc.) using Hanzomon Line and transfers.

## 8. Extra questions you probably have (but didn’t ask)

Here are quick answers to things most first‑timers in your situation wonder:

- **Language barrier?**  
  - Train announcements and signs on major lines are in English/Japanese; ticket machines usually have English buttons.[16][15]

- **Paying for trains?**  
  - Get a Suica/PASMO or use contactless where supported, then just tap in/out; no need to calculate fares every time.[17]

- **Tipping?**  
  - Japan generally does **not** do tipping. Prices include service.  

- **Smoking / weed / drugs?**  
  - Weed and many drugs are strictly illegal with harsh penalties. Do not bring or buy anything.  

- **Power outlets?**  
  - Type A (like U.S.), 100V. U.S. chargers work fine, just carry a small multi‑USB charger.  

- **Late night arrivals?**  
  - Trains thin out around midnight; if delayed significantly and you arrive very late, you may need an airport hotel or late‑night bus/taxi. Check your actual ETA when you land.
