# Riding High Above Tokyo: A Day on the Sky Hop Bus

If you’ve ever wanted Tokyo to introduce itself properly, the **Sky Hop Bus** is basically the city pulling up in a bright red open-top bus and saying, “Hop in, I’ll show you around.”[web:1] For your Saturday, April 4th adventure, this hop-on hop-off bus is a perfect early-in-the-trip activity: it orients you, hits a bunch of major neighborhoods, and lets you bail out whenever something shiny (read: shrine, tower, shopping street, or department store basement full of food) catches your eye.[web:1][web:6] Even better, all three routes start from the same hub near Tokyo Station, so it’s very hard to get truly lost—Tokyo for beginners, but with great city views.[web:1][web:11]

Sky Hop Bus Tokyo runs three main routes: the Red Route for Asakusa and Tokyo Skytree, the Blue Route for Tokyo Tower and Ginza, and the Green Route for Shinjuku and Shibuya.[web:1][web:6] Buses generally start around 9:00–9:30 and run until early evening, with departures roughly every 30–40 minutes depending on the route.[web:6][web:10] For early April, there are no special closures listed for Saturday the 4th, though there is a detour on the Red Route the next day (Sunday the 5th) due to the Spring Nihonbashi Festival—useful to know if you end up repeating the bus on Sunday.[web:1][web:9] You can buy 1-day or 2-day passes, and once you’re onboard, you’ll get multilingual audio commentary that does the heavy lifting of explaining what you’re passing while you just sit back and enjoy the view.[web:1][web:4][web:6]

The main departure point is the **Marunouchi Mitsubishi Building**, a few minutes’ walk from JR Tokyo Station’s Marunouchi side (the pretty red-brick side, not the neon-and-subways side).[web:1][web:11][web:15] The address is:  
- Marunouchi Mitsubishi Building, 2-5-2 Marunouchi, Chiyoda-ku, Tokyo 100-0005, Japan.[web:15]  
Approximate coordinates: **35.6813° N, 139.7658° E**.[web:15]  

Official site (timetables, route maps, tickets):  
- Sky Hop Bus Tokyo: https://skyhopbus.com[web:1][web:5]  
Good overviews and route details:  
- Klook (English): https://www.klook.com/en-US/activity/87165-sky-hop-on-hop-off-bus-tokyo/[web:6]  
- Headout (route timings): https://www.headout.com/hop-on-hop-off-bus-tours/hop-on-hop-off-bus-tokyo-e-19084/[web:10]  

Once you’ve had your fill of open-top sightseeing, the Marunouchi/Tokyo Station area turns out to be an excellent base camp for more exploring on foot. Below are several nearby spots that pair perfectly with your Sky Hop Bus day.

---

## Nearby Places Worth Hopping Off For

### **Tokyo Station Marunouchi Building**

Tokyo Station’s Marunouchi side is not just a transit hub; it’s a full-blown architectural flex.[web:11][web:15] The red-brick station building, originally opened in 1914 and painstakingly restored, looks like someone teleported a European railway palace into the middle of Japan’s business district.[web:11][web:15] In early April, the plazas in front are often bustling with people taking photos, and the evening illumination makes it especially atmospheric after your bus ride winds down.[web:11][web:15] Inside and underground, the station is a warren of souvenir shops, ekiben (station bento) stands, and little food streets—great for an easy dinner without straying far.

- Address: 1-9-1 Marunouchi, Chiyoda-ku, Tokyo 100-0005, Japan.[web:15]  
- Coordinates: **35.6814° N, 139.7671° E**.[web:15]  
- Info: https://www.jreast.co.jp/e/stations/e1039.html[web:15]  

---

### **Marunouchi Brick Square & Marunouchi Naka-dori**

Just a short walk from the bus stop, Marunouchi reveals its softer side along **Marunouchi Naka-dori**, a tree-lined street flanked by office towers, designer shops, and cafes.[web:15] The vibe is “Tokyo salaryman, but on their day off,” with seasonal illuminations in winter and a relaxed, almost European boulevard feel the rest of the year.[web:15] Early April might still catch lingering seasonal displays and window dressings geared toward hanami and the new school/fiscal year, so it’s a fun place to people-watch and window-shop after sitting on the bus all day.[web:15]

Marunouchi Brick Square, tucked just off this street, adds a cozy little courtyard framed by low-rise brick buildings with restaurants and terraces—perfect if you want a slightly more intimate break than the main station concourse.[web:15] It’s a nice contrast to the glass-and-steel skyline you’ve just been admiring from the top deck of the bus.

- Address (Brick Square): 2-6-1 Marunouchi, Chiyoda-ku, Tokyo 100-0005, Japan.[web:15]  
- Coordinates: **35.6798° N, 139.7646° E**.[web:15]  
- Info: https://marunouchi.com/ (Japanese, but great for maps and restaurant lists)[web:15]  

---

### **KITTE Marunouchi (Shopping, Food, and a Free Rooftop View)**

Right across from Tokyo Station’s Marunouchi exit, **KITTE** is a former central post office reborn as a stylish shopping and dining complex.[web:15] Inside you’ll find a mix of Japanese brands, regional food shops from all over the country, and restaurants that range from casual to quite polished—ideal if your group has wildly different cravings.[web:15] The real secret, though, is the free rooftop garden viewpoint where you can look straight across at Tokyo Station’s historic facade and watch the Shinkansen glide in and out like very punctual mechanical snakes.[web:15]

In early April, the rooftop vantage point is great for those “We are definitely in Tokyo” photos, especially with evening light and city towers in the background.[web:15] It’s also a good backup plan if the weather turns chilly after your time on the open-top deck; you can warm up with coffee and snacks without going far from the bus hub.

- Address: JP Tower/KITTE, 2-7-2 Marunouchi, Chiyoda-ku, Tokyo 100-0005, Japan.[web:15]  
- Coordinates: **35.6794° N, 139.7647° E**.[web:15]  
- Info: https://marunouchi.jp-kitte.jp/en/[web:15]  

---

### **The Imperial Palace Outer Gardens & Nijubashi Bridge**

From the Sky Hop Bus starting point, it’s an easy walk to the **Imperial Palace Outer Gardens**, where wide gravel paths, pine trees, and moats offer a surprising amount of calm in the middle of central Tokyo.[web:14][web:15] In early April, you may still catch late-blooming cherry trees or at least the fresh green of new leaves, which makes for a very “I am actually in Japan in spring” moment after all the cityscape views from the bus.[web:14][web:15] The iconic **Nijubashi Bridge**—a pair of arched bridges leading toward the inner palace—gives you that classic postcard shot and a bit of history to contrast with the glass skyscrapers just behind you.[web:14][web:15]

You can’t just stroll into the inner palace grounds whenever you like, but the outer areas are freely accessible and make a lovely walking loop from Marunouchi.[web:14][web:15] If you’ve been sitting most of the day, this is the ideal spot to stretch your legs, breathe, and remind yourself that Tokyo still has moats and feudal-era echoes tucked into its center.

- Address (Kokyo Gaien National Garden): 1-1 Chiyoda, Chiyoda-ku, Tokyo 100-8111, Japan.[web:14][web:15]  
- Coordinates (Nijubashi area): **35.6809° N, 139.7528° E**.[web:14][web:15]  
- Info: https://www.env.go.jp/garden/kokyogaien/english/index.html[web:14]  

---

### **Oazo & Yaesu Underground Shopping (For Rainy or Tired Legs Moments)**

If April decides to be moody (rain is not uncommon), the **Oazo** complex and the **Yaesu Underground** shopping streets give you a weatherproof way to keep exploring without going far.[web:15] Oazo, on the Marunouchi side, is a bright complex anchored by the massive Maruzen bookstore, which is a paradise if anyone in your group loves stationery, design books, or Japanese-language manga.[web:15] The Yaesu side, reached through the station, leads into a maze of underground corridors lined with casual restaurants, izakaya, and shops—a very local-feeling place to try a quick meal and absorb the after-work energy.[web:15]

This is not “highlight of the trip” territory, but it is “we’re jet-lagged, it’s raining, and we still want to feel like we’re in Tokyo without working too hard” territory, which is sometimes even better.[web:15] After a day of hop-on hop-off sightseeing, ducking underground for ramen or curry is a satisfying way to close the loop.

- Oazo Address: 1-6-4 Marunouchi, Chiyoda-ku, Tokyo 100-0005, Japan.[web:15]  
- Oazo Coordinates: **35.6831° N, 139.7655° E**.[web:15]  
- Yaesu Underground general area: under Yaesu side of Tokyo Station, around 2-1 Yaesu, Chuo-ku, Tokyo 104-0028, Japan.[web:15]  
- Coordinates (central Yaesu Underground): **35.6806° N, 139.7702° E**.[web:15]  
- Info (Tokyo Station city guide with underground links): https://www.tokyostationcity.com/english/[web:15]
