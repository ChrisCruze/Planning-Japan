<cherry_blossom_viewing>

# Cherry Blossoms Are the Reason You’re Going in April — Don’t Treat Them as Background

## Tokyo has a dozen world-class hanami (flower viewing) spots, and early April is the absolute peak. This isn’t a single activity — it should be woven into every free evening, every transit gap, every morning walk.

- **Chidorigafuchi (Imperial Palace moat)** — Widely considered the single most photogenic sakura spot in Tokyo. Rent rowboats surrounded by overhanging cherry trees along the moat. Illuminated at night. Located in Chiyoda, walkable from Tokyo Station or Kudanshita Station. Best at dusk when the lanterns come on.
- URL: [visit-chiyoda.tokyo/en/sakura](https://visit-chiyoda.tokyo/en/sakura)
- Lat/Long: 35.6912, 139.7505
- **Meguro River** — 4 kilometers of cherry trees lining both banks. Walk south from Nakameguro Station. Street food vendors and pop-up bars set up along the river during peak bloom. This is where young Tokyo goes for hanami — the vibe is electric at night.
- URL: [gotokyo.org/en/spot/397](https://www.gotokyo.org/en/spot/397/index.html)
- Lat/Long: 35.6439, 139.6989
- **Ueno Park** — The most famous and most crowded sakura spot. Over 1,000 trees. Groups picnic under the blossoms with tarps and bento boxes. Free. Located directly at Ueno Station (JR Yamanote Line). Combine with the Tokyo National Museum or Ueno Zoo.
- URL: [kensetsu.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/jimusho/toubuk/ueno](https://www.kensetsu.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/jimusho/toubuk/ueno/index_top.html)
- Lat/Long: 35.7146, 139.7732
- **Shinjuku Gyoen** — Massive garden with 1,000+ cherry trees across 144 acres. Entry 500 yen (~$3). Quieter and more manicured than Ueno. No alcohol allowed inside, which keeps it family-friendly and calm. Closest station: Shinjuku-gyoemmae (Marunouchi Line).
- URL: [fng.or.jp/shinjuku/en](https://fng.or.jp/shinjuku/en/)
- Lat/Long: 35.6852, 139.7100
- **Yoyogi Park** — Adjacent to Meiji Shrine and Harajuku. Large open lawns perfect for group picnics. Free. Less formal than the gardens — bring a blanket, konbini snacks, and chill.
- URL: [tokyo-park.or.jp/park/yoyogi](https://www.tokyo-park.or.jp/park/yoyogi/index.html)
- Lat/Long: 35.6717, 139.6949
- **Best fit:** Weave into Day 2 (bus route passes several), Day 5 (open day), Day 7 (before/after Tokyo Tower), and any evening walk.
- **Advance booking:** None required. Show up, walk, sit, enjoy.

> **Notes:** This appears in every Japan-in-April travel guide for a reason. The group should not leave Tokyo without at least one dedicated sakura walk — ideally Chidorigafuchi at dusk or Meguro River at night. Both are free and unforgettable. Shinjuku Gyoen closes at 6 PM so plan accordingly.

</cherry_blossom_viewing>

<senso_ji_temple>

# Senso-ji Is Tokyo’s Oldest Temple — And It’s Best at Dawn

## Located in Asakusa, this 7th-century Buddhist temple is the single most-visited spiritual site in the world. The approach street (Nakamise-dori) is lined with 90+ souvenir shops.

- **Location:** Asakusa, Taito. 5-minute walk from Asakusa Station (Ginza Line, Asakusa Line).
- URL: [senso-ji.jp/english](https://www.senso-ji.jp/english/)
- Lat/Long: 35.7148, 139.7967
- **The trick:** Arrive before 7 AM to experience the temple grounds nearly empty. By 10 AM it’s a wall of tourists. The five-story pagoda, the massive red lantern at Kaminarimon gate, and the incense cauldron at the main hall are all best photographed in early morning light.
- **Nakamise Shopping Street** runs 250 meters from the gate to the temple. Traditional snacks (ningyo-yaki, senbei), fans, chopsticks, trinkets. Most shops open by 9-10 AM. Cash preferred at smaller stalls.
- Lat/Long: 35.7118, 139.7963
- **Combine with:** The kimono experience on Day 7 happens in Asakusa — the girls will already be here. But the temple deserves its own visit earlier in the trip when the group has fresh energy.
- **Best fit:** Day 2 morning (before the hop-on hop-off bus) or Day 5 morning if staying in Tokyo for the open day. Also works as a quick evening visit — the temple is illuminated at night and the crowds thin after 7 PM.
- **Cost:** Free. Temple grounds are open 24/7. The main hall opens at 6 AM.
- **Advance booking:** None required.

> **Notes:** This is arguably the single most iconic “Tokyo photo” location. It appears repeatedly in the project research as a must-do. If the hotel is in Asakusa, this becomes a 5-minute morning walk. If not, it’s worth the train ride. The night illumination is a hidden gem most tourists miss.

</senso_ji_temple>

<meiji\_shrine>

# Meiji Shrine Feels Like a Forest Inside a Megacity

## A Shinto shrine surrounded by 170 acres of old-growth forest, located steps from Harajuku Station. The contrast between the quiet forest path and the chaos of Harajuku is jarring — and that’s the point.

- **Location:** Shibuya, adjacent to Harajuku and Yoyogi Park. 1-minute walk from Harajuku Station (JR Yamanote Line) or Meiji-jingumae Station (Chiyoda/Fukutoshin Lines).
- URL: [meijijingu.or.jp/en](https://www.meijijingu.or.jp/en/)
- Lat/Long: 35.6764, 139.6993
- **The approach:** A 10-minute walk through a towering torii gate and dense forest canopy before you reach the shrine itself. The gravel path, the silence, the filtered light — it’s a sensory reset after days of neon and crowds.
- **What to do:** Write a wish on an ema (wooden prayer plaque, 500 yen). Draw an omikuji (fortune slip). Watch for traditional Shinto wedding processions — they happen most weekends.
- **Best fit:** Day 2 afternoon. The hop-on hop-off bus stops in the Shibuya/Harajuku area. Get off, walk to Meiji Shrine, then cross into Harajuku’s Takeshita Street for the polar opposite experience. It’s a natural one-two punch.
- **Cost:** Free entry. Ema and omikuji are small optional purchases.
- **Advance booking:** None required. Open sunrise to sunset (approximately 5 AM - 6 PM in April).

> **Notes:** This pairs perfectly with a Harajuku and Shibuya walkthrough on the bus day. The proximity is the key — Meiji Shrine, Takeshita Street, and Shibuya Crossing are all within a 15-minute walk of each other. Do all three in sequence.

</meiji\_shrine>

<harajuku_takeshita_street>

# Takeshita Street Is Sensory Overload in the Best Way

## A 400-meter pedestrian lane packed with fashion boutiques, crepe stands, purikura photo booths, and candy-colored storefronts. This is teen Tokyo at maximum volume.

- **Location:** Harajuku, Shibuya. Directly across from Harajuku Station (JR Yamanote Line).
- URL: [gotokyo.org/en/spot/15](https://www.gotokyo.org/en/spot/15/index.html)
- Lat/Long: 35.6702, 139.7027
- **What you’ll find:** Cotton candy as big as your head, rainbow crepes from Marion Crepes (the original since 1977), vintage clothing shops, Daiso and other 100-yen stores, quirky accessory shops, and photo booth studios.
- **Time needed:** 30-60 minutes to walk the length. More if you’re shopping or eating.
- **Cat Street (nearby):** A parallel road with a more mature, hipster-design vibe — independent fashion labels, coffee shops, and streetwear. Walk both for the contrast.
- Lat/Long: 35.6679, 139.7044
- **Best fit:** Day 2 afternoon, immediately after Meiji Shrine. The bus route passes here. Also works anytime the group is in the Shibuya area.
- **Cost:** Free to walk. Budget 500-1,500 yen ($3-10) for snacks and photo booths.
- **Advance booking:** None required.

> **Notes:** This is the opposite energy of Meiji Shrine — do them back-to-back for the most memorable contrast of the trip. The crepes are a rite of passage. Skip the themed cafes (maid cafes, animal cafes) unless the group is specifically interested — they’re pricey for what you get.

</harajuku_takeshita_street>

<tsukiji_outer_market>

# Tsukiji Outer Market Is a Sushi Breakfast That Ruins Every Sushi Restaurant Back Home

## The old fish market’s inner wholesale floor moved to Toyosu in 2018, but the outer market stayed — and it’s better than ever. 400+ stalls selling the freshest seafood, tamagoyaki, street food, and kitchen goods.

- **Location:** Tsukiji, Chuo. 1-minute walk from Tsukiji Station (Hibiya Line) or 5-minute walk from Tsukijishijo Station (Oedo Line).
- URL: [tsukiji.or.jp/en](https://www.tsukiji.or.jp/en/)
- Lat/Long: 35.6654, 139.7707
- **Come hungry.** The move is to graze — a piece of tamago (sweet egg) here, a fresh tuna skewer there, uni (sea urchin) on rice from a stand, then finish with a sit-down sushi set. Budget 2,000-4,000 yen ($13-26) for a full morning of eating.
- **Arrive early.** Best stalls open at 5-6 AM. By 10 AM the narrow lanes are shoulder-to-shoulder. Weekday mornings are significantly less crowded than weekends.
- **Best fit:** Day 5 morning (the open day) or Day 4/Day 7 morning before the main activity starts. This is a perfect 7-9 AM activity that fills the pre-plan gap.
- **Nearby:** Walk 15 minutes south to Toyosu Market (tuna auctions) or north to Ginza (upscale shopping).
- Toyosu Market Lat/Long: 35.6466, 139.7811
- Ginza Lat/Long: 35.6717, 139.7649
- **Cost:** Free to enter. Food purchases vary.
- **Advance booking:** None required.

> **Notes:** This is the highest-impact food experience available that isn’t already on the itinerary. A sushi breakfast at Tsukiji is one of those “only in Japan” moments that the group will talk about for years. Best paired with the open day (Day 5) when there’s no 7 or 8 AM departure to compete with.

</tsukiji_outer_market>

<golden\_gai>

# Golden Gai Is 200 Bars Crammed Into Six Alleys — Each One Seats Five People

## Shinjuku’s Golden Gai is a postwar drinking district that survived demolition. Tiny themed bars, each with its own personality, stacked two stories high in lanes barely wide enough for two people to pass.

- **Location:** Kabukicho, Shinjuku. 5-minute walk from Shinjuku Station East Exit.
- URL: [gotokyo.org/en/spot/40](https://www.gotokyo.org/en/spot/40/index.html)
- Lat/Long: 35.6938, 139.7036
- **The experience:** Each bar seats roughly 5-10 people. Many have cover charges (300-1,000 yen / $2-6) plus drink prices. The bartender IS the entertainment — conversation, music, ambiance.
- **Look for “Tourist Welcome” signs.** Albatross (three stories, chandelier) and Death Match in Hell (horror-themed) are popular starting points.
- Albatross Bar Lat/Long: 35.6940, 139.7038
- **Time needed:** 1-2 hours. Bar-hop between 3-4 spots. Drinks average 500-800 yen ($3-5).
- **Best fit:** Day 3 or Day 4 evening, after returning from Kamakura or Mt. Fuji. Also perfect for Day 7 as the final night group outing.
- **Cost:** 2,000-4,000 yen ($13-26) per person for a few bars and drinks.
- **Advance booking:** None required. Just show up and wander.

> **Notes:** This is one of the most unique nightlife experiences in the world — nothing like it exists outside Japan. Not ideal for the whole group at once (bars are tiny) — split into pairs or trios and reconvene. Cash only at most bars.

</golden\_gai>

# Shibuya Sky Is the Best Rooftop View in Tokyo — And It’s on the Bus Route

## An open-air observation deck on the 47th floor of Shibuya Scramble Square, directly above Shibuya Crossing. Sunset slots sell out days in advance during cherry blossom season.

- **Location:** Shibuya Scramble Square, Shibuya. Directly connected to Shibuya Station.
- URL: [shibuya-sky.com/en](https://www.shibuya-sky.com/en/)
- Lat/Long: 35.6584, 139.7022
- **The view:** 360-degree panorama. Mt. Fuji visible on clear days. Tokyo Tower, Tokyo Skytree, Shinjuku skyline, Yoyogi Park’s green canopy. Open-air rooftop with no glass barriers.
- **Sunset is the move.** Book for 30-40 minutes before sunset. April sunset approximately 6:10-6:20 PM.
- **Best fit:** Day 2 late afternoon/evening or Day 5 if the open day stays in Tokyo.
- **Cost:** 2,000 yen (~$13) adults, 1,000 yen (~$6) children. Timed entry.
- **Advance booking:** Strongly recommended for sunset slots during April. Book at [shibuya-sky.com](https://www.shibuya-sky.com/en/).

> **Notes:** Sunset time slots during sakura season sell out. Book 1-2 weeks in advance minimum. Pairs naturally with the Day 2 Shibuya Crossing visit — they’re in the same building.

</shibuya\_sky>

- **Mitsukoshi Ginza (B2-B3)** — Upscale and extensive. Walkable from Tsukiji Market.
- URL: [mitsukoshi.mistore.jp/ginza](https://www.mitsukoshi.mistore.jp/ginza.html)
- Lat/Long: 35.6711, 139.7663
- **Takashimaya Shinjuku (B1-B2)** — Connected to Shinjuku Station south side.

- Lat/Long: 35.6874, 139.7022

- **Cost:** Free to browse. Items 300-1,500 yen ($2-10). Gift boxes 1,000-3,000 yen ($6-19).

> **Notes:** The insider souvenir strategy. Skip the airport duty-free (marked up, limited selection) and buy at depachika earlier. Daimaru at Tokyo Station is the most convenient for departure day.

# Tokyo Station Has an Entire Underground Street Dedicated to Ramen

## Tokyo Ramen Street houses 8 of Japan’s most acclaimed ramen shops in one corridor. No searching, no guessing — pick a style and eat.

- **Location:** Tokyo Station, Yaesu South Exit, B1 floor.
- URL: [tokyoeki-1bangai.co.jp/ramenstreet](https://www.tokyoeki-1bangai.co.jp/en/ramenstreet/)
- Lat/Long: 35.6788, 139.7688
- **Styles available:** Tonkotsu (pork bone, creamy), shoyu (soy sauce, clear), miso (rich, northern-style), shio (salt, delicate), and tsukemen (dipping noodles).
- **How to order:** Ticket vending machine (touchscreen, often with English). Buy ticket, hand to chef, sit at counter. Bowls arrive in 3-5 minutes.
- **Best fit:** Day 5 lunch, Day 8 last lunch before Narita (Tokyo Station is on the Narita Express route). Also works as a rain backup — everything is underground.
- **Time needed:** 20-40 minutes.
- **Cost:** 900-1,300 yen ($6-8) per bowl.
- **Advance booking:** None.

> **Notes:** For the last lunch on Day 8, this is hard to beat — inside the station the group may already be passing through to catch the Narita Express. Every shop has earned its slot through competitions and critical acclaim.
