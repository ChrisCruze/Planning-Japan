### Section 6: Getting There and Insider Tips

Beyond your wardrobe, your daily daypack needs to be strategic. To make your daily transit as smooth as possible, I have put together a quick listicle of my top insider packing tips that most first-timers miss:

* **Tip One: Pack a pocket hand towel.** Most public restrooms in Japanese train stations and parks are impeccably clean, but they do not provide paper towels or air dryers. You need a small cotton handkerchief in your bag, just like the locals carry.
* **Tip Two: Bring a dedicated coin purse.** While Japan is embracing credit cards much more widely, it remains a cash-heavy society for small purchases. You will quickly accumulate a mountain of heavy 100 and 500 yen coins from vending machines and temple offerings.
* **Tip Three: Set up your digital wallet before you fly.** Add a digital Suica or Pasmo card directly to your Apple Wallet or Google Pay, and download an eSIM so you can bypass the long connectivity queues in the arrival hall.
* **Tip Four: Forward your heavy luggage.** Do not drag your massive checked bags across the country. Use the [Yamato Transport Ta-Q-Bin service](https://www.global-yamato.com/en/hands-free-travel/) at the airport to ship your large suitcase directly to your hotel for about twenty dollars, allowing you to ride the trains completely hands-free with just a daypack.
