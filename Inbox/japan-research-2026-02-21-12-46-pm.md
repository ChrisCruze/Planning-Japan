## 🍜 Where to Eat & Drink

## 🏛️ What to See & Do

**Wabona Toyosu Fish Market Tour** ([🔗 Website](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://wabona.jp/))
Tourists are largely banned from the inner wholesale floors of the massive Toyosu Fish Market. To get down onto the floor where 300-pound tunas are being sliced and auctioned, you need to book an exclusive local guide like Wabona. It's a fascinating, high-speed environment.

**Sumo Wrestling Morning Practice** Instead of fighting for expensive tournament tickets, book a morning training session (Asageiko) through a concierge or tour platform. For around $200, you get to sit in absolute silence inside a traditional dojo and watch 300-pound athletes slam into each other from five feet away.

**Tokyo Rent-A-Ossan (Rent a Grandpa)** ([🔗 Website](https://ossanrental.thebase.in/))
Japan has a unique "rental" culture designed to combat loneliness. You can rent a middle-aged man for about $7 an hour to give you life advice, act as a tour guide, or just accompany you to a J-pop concert.

**Golden Gai Karaoke** ([📍 Maps](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://www.google.com/maps/search/%3Fapi%3D1%26query%3DGolden%2BGai))
Tucked away in Shinjuku, this area features six narrow alleys packed with over 200 micro-bars. Many seat no more than 5 or 6 people. Find one that welcomes foreigners (look for English menus outside), buy a drink, and scream karaoke with local salarymen.

**Hanazono Shrine at Night** ([📍 Maps](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://www.google.com/maps/search/%3Fapi%3D1%26query%3DHanazono%2BShrine))
A striking red Shinto shrine right in the middle of Shinjuku’s chaos. It’s open 24/7 and takes on a magical, cinematic quality when it rains at night.

## 🚶 Neighborhoods to Explore

* **Shinjuku**: The neon beating heart of Tokyo. Home to Kabukicho (the red-light district overseen by a giant Godzilla head), Golden Gai, and endless arcades. It’s dystopian, overwhelming, and incredible.
* **Harajuku**: The center of Japan's extreme youth culture. Walk down Takeshita Street to see wild cosplay, eat rainbow street food, and visit bizarre animal cafes (like **mipig cafe**).
* **Shibuya**: Just south of Harajuku. You must walk the iconic Shibuya Crossing. It’s organized chaos at its finest.

## 🚄 Getting Around

* **The Golden Rule of Transit**: Shut up. Literally. The train system in Tokyo transports millions of people a day, yet the subway cars are library-quiet. Talking on the phone or having loud conversations is considered incredibly rude.
* **Shibuya Station**: It is a labyrinth. Give yourself an extra 15 minutes just to figure out which exit you need to take.
* **Vending Machines**: There is roughly one vending machine for every 23 people in Japan. You will never go thirsty. Keep loose 100-yen coins handy for iced coffees, hot teas, and strange soups.

## 💡 Pro Tips & Practical Info

* **The Loneliness Epidemic**: As you walk through areas like Kabukicho, you’ll notice men and women standing with signs. Many are promoting maid cafes, host clubs, or offering companionship. Japan is hyper-individualistic, and "transactional intimacy" (which is often just paying someone to listen to you) is a massive industry. Be respectful and observant.
* **Love Hotels**: Found heavily in areas like Shibuya's Dogenzaka or Shinjuku. These are themed hotels rented by the hour to give young adults (who often live in multi-generational homes) a place for privacy.
* **Trash Cans**: There are virtually no public trash cans in Tokyo. You are expected to carry your trash with you until you get back to your hotel or find a convenience store.
* **High-Tech Toilets**: Embrace the bidet! Japanese toilets will warm the seat, play music to mask sounds, and spray you clean. You will miss them deeply when you leave.

* **Lunch**: Eat a rainbow hot dog at **Le Shiner**, then cuddle with micro-pigs at **mipig cafe**.
* **Afternoon**: Walk down to Shibuya. Experience the Shibuya Crossing.
* **Dinner**: Slurp a bowl of ramen in absolute isolation at **Ichiran Ramen**.

**Day 2: Tradition & Tech**

* **Morning**: Wake up early for an exclusive **Toyosu Fish Market** tour, or sit in on a **Sumo Wrestling Dojo** practice.
* **Lunch**: Head to Nihonbashi for lunch served by a robot at the **DAWN Avatar Robot Cafe**.
* **Afternoon**: Explore the upscale streets of Ginza. Wait in line for the legendary chicken broth at **Ginza Kagari**.
* **Dinner**: Treat yourself to a 17-year masterclass in raw fish at **Sushi Kenichiro**.

**Day 3: The Neon Night**

* **Morning/Afternoon**: Rent a local "Ossan" (Grandpa) to show you around his favorite local haunts and grab some Mt. Fuji **Kakigori** shaved ice.
* **Evening**: Head to Shinjuku. Walk down Godzilla Road in Kabukicho.
* **Late Night**: Visit the **Hanazono Shrine** under the neon lights, then squeeze into a tiny bar in **Golden Gai** to sing karaoke until the trains start running again.

## 🔍 Beyond the Video: Additional Recommendations

If you want to round out this weird, wonderful trip, you absolutely cannot miss these spots that the creator didn't include:

1. **TeamLab Planets (Toyosu)**: Since you are already heading to Toyosu for the fish market, book tickets months in advance for this immersive, barefoot digital art museum. You walk through knee-deep water filled with digital koi fish and wander through infinite crystal universes.
2. **Senso-ji Temple (Asakusa)**: To balance out the neon dystopian vibes of Shinjuku, head to Asakusa. Senso-ji is Tokyo’s oldest and most significant Buddhist temple. The street leading up to it (Nakamise-dori) is the best place in the city to buy traditional souvenirs and street snacks.
3. **Omoide Yokocho (Memory Lane / Piss Alley)**: If you love the tiny-bar vibe of Golden Gai, you need to visit this alleyway in Shinjuku. It’s packed with miniature, smoke-filled yakitori stands where chefs grill chicken skewers over charcoal right in front of you.
4. **Convenience Store (Konbini) Culture**: Do not sleep on 7-Eleven, Lawson, or FamilyMart. They are culinary destinations. Grab an egg salad sandwich from 7-Eleven, Famichiki (fried chicken) from FamilyMart, and a Premium Roll Cake from Lawson.
