### Section 5: Planning for April 2026

Let's talk about the dates: April 3rd through April 10th. Keyla, Yalisa, and Leili have accidentally—or perhaps intentionally—hit the absolute jackpot for Tokyo's cherry blossom season.

While the bloom shifts slightly every year, the first week of April is historically peak time for the *somei yoshino* blossoms in Tokyo. Because the hotel is located right at the Imperial Palace, you are just a ten-minute walk from Chidorigafuchi Moat. This is arguably Tokyo's most breathtaking sakura spot, where hundreds of cherry trees drape their branches directly into the water. In the evenings, they illuminate the trees, creating a magical atmosphere. You can check the historical bloom dates and upcoming 2026 predictions on [Japan Guide's Cherry Blossom Forecast](https://www.japan-guide.com/sakura/).

Weather-wise, early April in Tokyo is crisp and brilliant. You are looking at daytime highs around 60 degrees Fahrenheit and chillier evenings. You'll want layers—a light jacket or a trench coat is perfect. Also, because you are missing Golden Week, which starts later in April, you avoid the absolute peak of domestic travel chaos, though the city will still be globally busy for the blossoms.

### Section 6: Getting There and Insider Tips

Getting to the hotel is remarkably straightforward. The property is a mere two-minute walk from Exit 1 of Hanzomon Station on the Tokyo Metro Hanzomon Line.

Now, here is a vital logistical tip: The Hanzomon Line is a subway line, not a JR (Japan Railway) line. This means that if the group is planning to buy a JR Pass, they cannot use it to get to their immediate hotel stop. Instead, everyone will need a digital IC card—like a Suica or Pasmo—loaded onto their Apple Wallets or picked up as a physical card upon arrival. You can map out all your daily routes using the incredibly reliable [Navitime Transit Planner](https://japantravel.navitime.com/en/area/jp/route/).

As for an insider tip you won't find in standard guides: because the rooms at the Nest Hotel are quite small, large suitcases can be a nightmare. I highly recommend using the *Takuhaibin* (luggage forwarding service) if you are traveling from the airport or another city. Have your heavy bags shipped directly to the hotel lobby for about twenty bucks, and just travel to Hanzomon with a small overnight backpack.

### Section 7: Nearby and Worth Exploring

Because Hanzomon is so central, you have some incredible spots practically in your backyard.

Just a short walk north is the **Chidorigafuchi Green Way**. As mentioned, this is your premier cherry blossom spot. Renting a rowboat here during peak bloom is a bucket-list Tokyo experience. You can find out more about the boat rentals on the [Visit Chiyoda official site](https://visit-chiyoda.tokyo/app/en/spot/detail/446).

A bit further east, you can walk to the **Imperial Palace East Gardens**. This is a massive, meticulously manicured landscape built on the former site of Edo Castle's innermost circles. It's free to enter and offers a beautiful morning walk.

For dining, while Hanzomon is quiet at night, the neighboring area of Kojimachi (one subway stop away or a short walk) has fantastic, unpretentious lunch spots and izakayas catering to local office workers. You can use [Tabelog](https://tabelog.com/en/tokyo/A1308/A130803/) to find highly-rated, highly-authentic noodle shops hidden in the nearby alleys.
