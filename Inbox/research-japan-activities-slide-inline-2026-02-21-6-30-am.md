### 🏪 Convenience Store Culture — Don’t Skip

Japan’s konbini are exceptional and nothing like back home:

- **7-Eleven, FamilyMart, Lawson** — open 24 hours, on every block
- Onigiri (rice balls): ¥130 each (~**$0.84**) — fresh, filling, different flavors daily
- Hot nikuman pork buns, egg salad sandwiches, hot oden
- Perfect for cheap daily breakfast (~¥300–500 per person, ~**$1.95–$3.25**)
- Useful for late-night snacks after long days out

### 🍱 Day Trip Food Notes

**Kamakura:** Bring cash — many stalls and restaurants are cash-only. Try shirasu (baby whitebait) rice bowls — a Kamakura specialty. Komachi Street has matcha soft serve and traditional sweets.

**Mt. Fuji:** Pack snacks from konbini the night before. The 5th Station has a food hall with curry and noodles. Kawaguchiko town has restaurants near the lake.

## Packing & Practical Tips

### What to Pack for April

- **Layers** — mornings and evenings are cool (10–12°C); afternoons are pleasant
- **Jacket** — mandatory for Mt. Fuji day; it is significantly colder at elevation
- **Comfortable sneakers** — Keyla’s explicit instruction for Kamakura and Mt. Fuji; expect 15,000–25,000 steps every day
- Small day backpack for each person (don’t bring big bags on day trips)
- Portable umbrella — light rain is possible
- **Passport** — required on Mt. Fuji day (Keyla’s note; likely for bus tour)
- **Credit card** — bring on Mt. Fuji day (Keyla’s note)

### Japan Etiquette Essentials

- **Remove shoes** when entering homes, ryokans, and some traditional restaurants — look for a raised entry floor
- **No eating while walking** on streets — find a spot and eat there
- **Trash cans are rare** — carry your trash until you find a bin (konbini have them)
- **Cash is still king** — carry ¥10,000–20,000 (~**$64–$129**) at all times; many places don’t take cards
- **Be quiet on trains** — no phone calls, keep voices low; the quiet is striking at first
- **Always queue** — Japan takes lines seriously; never cut regardless of how impatient you feel
- **Bow when greeted** — a small nod works perfectly for tourists

### Money & Payments

- **ATMs:** Use 7-Eleven ATMs — reliably accept foreign cards, English menus
- **Suica/Pasmo:** Load ¥10,000 (~$64.50) at arrival; swipe for trains, buses, konbini, vending machines
- **Exchange rate:** **$1 = ¥155** (as of Feb 20, 2026) — favorable for American travelers
- **Tax-free shopping:** Show passport at department stores for purchases over ¥5,000 (~$32.25) — saves 10%

### Connectivity

- **Pocket Wi-Fi rental** — rent at Narita on arrival or pre-order online. ~$5–8/day; group shares one device. Most reliable option.
- **SIM card alternative** — individual data SIMs at airport kiosks (IIJmio, Mobal). Good if people are splitting off.
- Download **offline Google Maps** for Tokyo before departing the US — critical when Wi-Fi cuts out
