## **Kamakura — History, Temples & Highlights**

Kamakura is a historic coastal city in Kanagawa Prefecture once ruled by powerful samurai and shoguns, often referred to as the “Kyoto of the East.” It’s filled with **Buddhist temples, Shinto shrines, scenic streets, and cultural symbols dating back centuries**. ([Japan Travel][1])

---

### **1. Kōtoku-in & The Great Buddha**

📍 **Kotoku-in Temple — Great Buddha (Daibutsu)**
The massive bronze statue was cast around **1252** and is one of Japan’s **most iconic historical monuments**. At about 13.3 meters tall, it’s the **second tallest outdoor bronze Buddha in Japan** — and you can even **enter inside the statue**. ([Japan Travel][2])

👉 Learn more: [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C5%8Dtoku-in](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C5%8Dtoku-in)

---

### **2. Hase-dera Temple**

📍 **Hase-dera (Hase Temple)**
This temple is known for its **large wooden statue of the eleven-faced Bodhisattva Kannon**, beautiful hillside gardens, and great views overlooking Sagami Bay. The temple ground even includes a **small cave and shrine areas**. ([Wikipedia][3])

👉 Learn more: [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hase-dera_%28Kamakura%29](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hase-dera_%28Kamakura%29)

---

### **Optional Spots in Kamakura**

If time allows — especially walking or free time:

* **Tsurugaoka Hachiman-gū Shrine** – the most important Shinto shrine in Kamakura, spanning peaceful grounds and ponds (often included in walking tours). ([GetYourGuide][4])
* **Komachi Street** – a lively pedestrian shopping street lined with shops and local snacks near Kamakura Station. ([THAT BACKPACKER][5])
* **Enoden Railway** – a scenic vintage train line along the coast linking Kamakura ↔ Enoshima. ([Japan Guide][6])

*These are not necessarily part of your booked tour stops, but they help fill in local context.*

---

## **Afternoon — Enoshima Island**

After exploring Kamakura’s temples, you’ll move toward the coast and visit **Enoshima Island** — a scenic coastal island connected to the mainland by a bridge. ([Japan Travel][7])

Enoshima blends **modern seaside charm with traditional shrines, ocean views, and natural attractions**. You’ll often see people walking across the pedestrian bridge, exploring cafes, sea views, and seaside pathways. ([Japan Travel][7])

---

### **3. Enoshima Sea Candle & Samuel Cocking Garden**

📍 **Enoshima Sea Candle (Observation Tower)**
Also known as the Enoshima Lighthouse Observation Tower, this striking lighthouse offers **wide panoramic views** of the coast and ocean — on a clear day even **Mt. Fuji in the distance**. ([鎌倉観光完全ガイド | 音声ガイド×地図でめぐる鎌倉観光][8])

The observation area sits within the **Samuel Cocking Garden**, a beautiful hillside garden with floral displays, pathways, and viewpoints on the island’s summit. ([江の島シーキャンドル・江の島サムエル・コッキング苑][9])

👉 Learn more: [https://enoshima-seacandle.com/](https://enoshima-seacandle.com/)

---

### **4. Enoshima Shrine & Iwaya Sea Caves**

📍 **Enoshima Shrine**
A historic shrine complex on Enoshima honoring the goddess Benzaiten and filled with traditional walkways and serene atmosphere — a local spiritual stop before ocean views. ([Japan Travel][7])

📍 **Iwaya Caves**
Historic sea caves carved into the rock, with paths and small scenic areas. They’re a unique geological feature on the island and popular with visitors. ([Japan Travel][7])

Outdoor experience, ocean breeze, and island exploration are the big highlights here.

---
